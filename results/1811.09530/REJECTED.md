# Rejected at Step 0: arXiv:1811.09530

Paper: Gerhard Pfister and Andreas Steenpass,
[*On the Primary Decomposition of Some Determinantal Hyperedge Ideal*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09530)
(v2, 17 July 2019).

## Decision

No module is shipped. The paper establishes a difficult computation for **one
fixed ideal**, but it does not supply a scalable problem family for which a
bounded, cheaply executable primary-decomposition witness can be generated.
The native proposal therefore fails **G (scalable generation), V (cheap exact
witness verification), and H on Track A**. I also considered **Track B** before
rejecting it: Section 3.4 contains a genuine large mechanical/compact gap for
one saturation check, but that shortcut does not produce or certify the full
primary decomposition.

This is a Step 0 rejection. No generator was written, so there is no retained
`rejected_gen_1811_09530.py`, self-test report, or oracle transcript.

## What the paper actually proves

Section 2 fixes the ring

```text
Q[x1,...,x12,y1,...,y12,z1,...,z12]
```

and one determinantal hyperedge ideal `I`, generated by sixteen specified
`3 x 3` minors of one `3 x 12` matrix. This is not an `n`-parameter definition:
the `3 x 4` index grid, 36 variables, and sixteen generators are fixed.

Theorem 4.1 proves that this particular ideal has the unique irredundant
primary decomposition

```text
I = P1 intersection P2,
```

where both components are prime and all three ideals have Krull dimension 26.
`P1` is generated by every `3 x 3` minor of the matrix. The second component is
given by a computed list of 44 polynomials. Its first sixteen generators are
those of `I`; its other 28 generators have degree 12. Section 4 gives partial
combinatorial observations about those degree-12 polynomials, but ends by saying
that their structure, including their coefficients, is not fully understood.
Thus the paper gives neither a formula for `P2` at growing size nor a theorem
asserting the same decomposition for a parameterized class.

## Why the prior-triage proposal does not work

The proposal was to choose primary ideals, form their intersection, and ask for
the decomposition. Forming an arbitrary intersection does not by itself provide
an executable certificate that the displayed components are primary or that
the submitted list is the irredundant primary decomposition. These are separate
claims:

- Mutual ideal containment requires ideal-membership representations or
  Groebner-basis reductions, not comparison of finite generator lists.
- Primariness is a universal condition. The paper checks the difficult component
  with localization, maximal-ideal testing, and repeated saturations in Algorithm
  1 (Proposition 3.2 and Corollary 3.3); it does not give a small certificate
  language whose verifier can replace those computations.
- Irredundancy and completeness require ruling out missing or embedded
  components. Merely multiplying or intersecting planted factors does not certify
  that stronger statement unless the generator restricts to a special class
  whose decomposition is already mechanically exposed.

Restricting the proposal to coordinate or monomial primary ideals would make G
and V easy, but would discard the paper's determinantal ideal and its
localization/saturation problem. Such instances are decomposed by direct support
inspection or standard monomial-ideal algorithms; they provide neither the
paper's hard regime nor a paper-backed Track B compression.

## Mechanical cost and compact route

The paper reports the relevant costs unusually clearly:

| computation | reported mechanical cost | compact route |
|---|---:|---:|
| Standard GTZ and Shimoyama--Yokoyama primary decomposition of `I` (Sections 1 and 3) | neither Singular implementation finished within **one month** | none supplied |
| Longest necessary saturation in the primality check of `P2` (Section 4) | **117 hours** on an Intel i7-6700 up to 4 GHz | none supplied |
| A symmetry-equivalent saturation (Sections 3.4 and 4) | almost **500 hours** when actually run | apply `sigma=(1 4)(2 5)(3 6)` to each of `x_i,y_i,z_i`: **9 variable transpositions plus one transported equality** |

The last row is a real Track B gap, not a reason to dismiss the paper merely
because an algorithm exists. Section 3.4 observes that if an automorphism
`phi` fixes `P`, sends `c_j` to `c_i`, and `P=P:<c_j^infinity>` is already known,
then applying `phi` transports that equality to `c_i`. In the fixed example the
displayed permutation avoids the nearly 500-hour duplicate computation.

However, this compact route certifies only the target saturation **after** the
source saturation and the invariance of `P2` are known. It does not find `P2`,
prove the other saturations, establish `I=P1 intersection P2`, or establish that
both components are prime. Turning it into “find the hidden permutation” would
be a newly designed polynomial-automorphism/isomorphism puzzle. The paper gives
no growing distribution or hardness result for that search problem, and it
would not be a primary-decomposition certificate.

For the full decomposition there is no short route to compare with the
mechanical one. The exact Singular-readable `P2` appended to the TeX source is
about **384,037 characters over 9,591 lines** including its declaration, versus
the task's 2,000-character and 256-atom answer caps. The source contains roughly
9,500 monomial lines; even `g17` alone has 216 monomials and `g19` has 252, as
Section 4 states. A solver would have to reproduce this computed table (and
still would not have supplied executable primality and completeness
certificates). Calling that table by the name `P2` would make the checker trust
hidden ground truth rather than inspect a witness.

## Gate assessment

| requirement | result |
|---|---|
| **G -- generatable** | **Fails for an unlimited native family.** Theorem 4.1 supplies one fixed certified instance. Relabelling its 36 variables only transforms that instance and must collapse to one canonical key under G8. The paper has no growing version of Theorem 4.1 or construction of `P2`. |
| **H -- Track A** | **Fails.** The month/117-hour timings concern one instance, not a theorem or measured hard distribution. No parameter regime is stated from which infinitely many hard instances can be sampled. |
| **H -- Track B** | **Fails for the full problem.** The 500-hour-versus-10-action symmetry shortcut applies to one redundant saturation, not to production of the decomposition witness. The full output has no compact route and exceeds the answer cap by about two orders of magnitude. |
| **V -- exact witness checking** | **Fails for the proposed answer.** Generator-list comparison cannot verify equality of ideals, primariness, or completeness. Executing Algorithm 1/Groebner reductions would repeat the research-scale computation; trusting Theorem 4.1 or a hidden stored `P2` would violate the witness rule. |
| **G7 -- scaling** | **Fails.** The native object has fixed 36 variables. Doubling `n` is not licensed by a theorem in the paper. |
| **G8 -- diversity** | **Fails for transformation-only generation.** Row, row-block, and column-class permutations are explicitly symmetries in Section 2, so relabelled copies are the same canonical problem. |
| **G9(c) -- answer size** | **Fails.** The supplied `P2` alone is approximately 384k characters and thousands of monomials, before adding an executable primality/equality certificate. |

## What would reopen this decision

A later result could make this paper usable if it supplied either (1) a
parameterized analogue of Theorem 4.1 with a compositional finite certificate
for both components, or (2) a compact exact description of the 28 degree-12
generators together with bounded, executable certificates of ideal equality and
primality. Without such a result, extending the `3 x 4` pattern, inventing
monomial components, or posing a hidden-permutation puzzle would be new
mathematics or a convenience analogue rather than a verified generator from
this paper.
