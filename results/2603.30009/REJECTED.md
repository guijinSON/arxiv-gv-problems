# Rejected at Step 0: the paper's scalable labelings are direct formulas

Paper: Mukti Acharya, [*Construction of additively graceful signed
graphs-I*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.30009), arXiv:2603.30009v1 (2026).

## Decision

No problem generator is shipped. The prior-triage proposal correctly identified
an exactly checkable graph-labeling witness, but the full paper does **not** give
a general structure-preserving transformation from an arbitrary graph. Its
scalable results are stars, bistars, and fixed `K3`/`K4` cores with pendant
leaves, and every proof writes down the vertex labels explicitly.

The resulting family can satisfy **G** by theorem-backed construction and **V**
by exact substitution, but it fails **H on both tracks**:

- **Track A fails.** On the distribution generated by any Section 2 theorem,
  recognizing the fixed core and applying the displayed labeling is a linear-time
  algorithm. The paper contains no worst-case or distributional hardness theorem
  for additively graceful labeling, much less for these exposed star-like objects.
- **Track B fails.** The paper's formula is already the compact route. A solver
  scans the signed edges, recognizes the star or constant-size clique core, and
  assigns a consecutive interval to the pendant leaves. The mechanical method
  and the by-hand shortcut have the same linear length; there is nothing to
  compress.

This is an H failure, not a witness-rule failure. A submitted vertex-label vector
is concrete, and a checker can verify injectivity, bounds, and all induced signed
edge labels exactly. Per the task's Step-0 stop rule, Steps 1--4 were not run and
there is no generator, self-test report, README, or oracle transcript.

## Exact definition and what produces the certificate

Definition 1.5 calls a labeling of a `(p,m,n)` signed graph additively graceful
when it is an injection

```text
f : V -> {0,1,...,m + ceil((n+1)/2)}
```

such that a negative edge `uv` receives `f(u)+f(v)` and the negative-edge
labels are exactly `1,...,n`, while a positive edge receives
`abs(f(u)-f(v))` and the positive-edge labels are exactly `1,...,m`.

The certificate-producing algorithms are the proofs themselves:

- Theorem 2.1 labels an all-negative `P3` by `1,0,2` and labels the `m`
  positive pendant vertices at the final vertex by `3,...,m+2`.
- Theorem 2.2 classifies a signed star: it is additively graceful exactly when
  it has one negative edge. Its sufficiency proof labels the center `1`, the
  negative leaf `0`, and the positive leaves `2,...,m+1`.
- Theorem 2.3 gives the same kind of consecutive formula for a bistar with one
  negative pendant edge.
- Theorems 2.4 and 2.5 use a fixed triangle core and again attach consecutively
  labeled positive leaves.
- Theorem 2.6 labels an all-negative `K4` core by `0,1,2,4`, whose six sums
  are `1,...,6`, and assigns labels `5,...,m+4` to the positive leaves.
- Figures 7 and 8 contain only finitely many further fixed-core examples.

Section 3 supplies three small non-divisible-sum examples (`G(2,3)`, `G(6,3)`,
and `G(6,4)`) and ends by asking which parameters work. It provides neither a
scalable construction nor a hard parameter regime. Section 4 likewise asks
whether the order and size of the constructed graphs can be increased.

Thus the answer to the Step-0 discriminating question is: **the certificate is
produced by a displayed linear formula, at linear cost in the number of pendant
vertices**.

## Mechanical cost versus compact route

Use Theorem 2.2 at a representative would-be shipping point of `m=64` positive
edges. The instance has 66 vertices, 65 edges, and a 66-integer answer, so it is
comfortably within the output cap and large enough to expose the scaling issue.
Assume the vertices and edge list have been randomly permuted, the strongest
obfuscation that preserves this theorem family.

The natural domain-standard algorithm is not factorial enumeration. It is the
paper's star formula after recognizing the relabeled star:

1. inspect 65 edge signs to locate the unique negative edge;
2. use at most two endpoint comparisons with a saved positive edge to identify
   which negative-edge endpoint is the center;
3. assign `1` to the center and `0` to the negative leaf (2 assignments);
4. for each of 64 positive edges, compare one endpoint with the center and
   assign its other endpoint the next value in `2,...,65` (128 operations).

This totals at most **197 primitive exact inspections, comparisons, and
assignments**, is `O(m)`, and directly returns a valid witness. The compact
route available to a solver who sees the structure is **the identical 197-step
scan and consecutive assignment**. Its mechanical-to-compact ratio is 1.
Keeping the construction roles in the vertex names only makes both routes
shorter; increasing `m` lengthens both routes and the answer together.

As a sanity check, a standard-library CPython implementation was run for
20,000 recoveries of one randomly vertex-relabeled and edge-shuffled instance.
It returned a verifier-accepted labeling every time and averaged **71.309
microseconds per recovery** at `m=64`. At the 256-atom ceiling (`m=254`) the
same routine averaged **218.530 microseconds** over 20,000 recoveries. These
timings are machine-dependent; the 197-operation count and the 1:1
mechanical-to-compact ratio are the portable evidence.

For Theorem 2.6 the situation is unchanged: degree inspection identifies the
unique pendant-leaf hub and the three other `K4` vertices, after which the
printed core labels `0,1,2,4` and a consecutive leaf interval solve the instance
in `O(m)`. Random vertex relabeling does not conceal degrees, edge signs, or the
constant-size core.

Quoting a factorial brute-force search as the Track-B mechanical method would be
dishonest for this generated distribution: the paper itself supplies the linear
algorithm that applies to every emitted instance.

## Independent guess-resistance failure

Theorem 2.2 also cannot pass G4 under the answer cap. With `m` positive edges,
the label range and the vertex set both have size `m+2`, so the broad injective
candidate language contains `(m+2)!` labelings. Exactly `m!` are valid: the
center is forced to `1`, the negative leaf is forced to `0`, and the positive
leaves may receive `2,...,m+1` in any order. Hence even this insufficiently
structure-aware density is

```text
m! / (m+2)! = 1 / ((m+1)(m+2)).
```

At `m=64` it is **1/4290 = 2.331e-4**. At the largest possible full labeling
under the 256-atom cap, `m=254`, it is still **1/65280 = 1.532e-5**, above the
required `1e-6` threshold. A genuinely structure-aware candidate generator must
enforce the immediately deducible center and negative-leaf labels; it then
samples a permutation of `2,...,m+1`, and **every candidate verifies**, giving
success probability 1.

The forcing argument is elementary. The negative edge must sum to 1, so its
endpoints are labeled 0 and 1. If the center were 0, the positive edge labeled 1
would require another vertex labeled 1, violating injectivity. Therefore the
center is 1 and the negative leaf is 0; all remaining labels can be assigned
arbitrarily to the positive leaves.

## Diversity failure of the proposed transformation

At fixed `m`, Theorem 2.2 produces one signed-star isomorphism class. Permuting
vertex numbers or reordering edges creates the same problem and must have the
same `canonical_key`; it cannot provide 20 unrelated instances for G8. The other
proved scalable constructions likewise have one fixed signed core plus
indistinguishable pendant leaves for each `m`. Mixing all of Sections 2's few
templates still gives only finitely many isomorphism types at a fixed size, not
an unlimited seed-indexed supply of distinct problems.

An inverse generator could start from an arbitrary label permutation and choose
one edge for each required sum or difference. That would meet G and V, but it is
not a transformation or hardness regime proved in this paper. The private random
permutation would also provide no solver-visible compact route, so it would not
qualify for Track B; the paper gives no average-case theorem that would justify
Track A.

| Requirement | Result | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| G -- known certificate by construction | Possible in isolation | Theorem 2.2 and the other Section 2 proofs print valid label formulas |

| H -- Track B no-tool compression | **Fail** | Mechanical and compact routes are the same 197-step linear method at `m=64` |

No oracle failure or fabricated gate report can repair a deterministic linear
solver that succeeds on every generated instance.
