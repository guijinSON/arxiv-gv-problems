## What the family is

The source is Li An-Ping, [*Birthday attack to discrete logarithm*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.1400).  The paper forms sets of powers of public keys `y_i=g^x_i`; a collision gives an exact linear equation in the unknown exponents modulo `|G|`, and enough independent collisions give a solvable linear system.  An instance here supplies a prime-order cyclic subgroup, 32 public group elements, and 32 composed collision identities as `A x = b (mod q)`.  The solver returns the least nonnegative simultaneous discrete logs.

The construction samples `x` first, samples nonzero vectors `u,v`, and publishes `A=I+uv^T`, `b=Ax`, and `y_i=g^x_i`.  Thus the witness is known by inverse generation, not by solving the published system.  `verify` independently checks every `g^x_i=y_i` and every row of `A x=b` with exact integer arithmetic; it never reads `inst["answer"]`.

This is a paper-licensed representation rather than native birthday-search coverage.  The raw power sets and collision-finding stage have been composed away; the retained objects are the cyclic group, its public elements, and exactly the modular linear system that Section 2 says the collisions produce.

## Why this is Track B

The paper itself rules out a Track A claim.  Immediately after its collision equations, Section 2 says to recover the keys by solving the independent linear system.  The introduction also names baby-step–giant-step, Pohlig–Hellman, and index calculus, and the closing paragraph points out the easy smooth-order decomposition into subgroups.

Exact Gaussian elimination is the reference algorithm: it is `O(n^3)`, solves 8/8 shipping instances, and in the final audit averaged 0.001361 seconds, 23,376 exact field operations, and 636 Euclidean divisions.  That speed is expected and disclosed.

The no-tool compression is the matrix invariant.  Factoring `A-I=u'v'^T` gives

`x = b - u' * (v'^T b)/(1+v'^T u') (mod q)`.

At shipping `n=32`, factor recovery and this formula take at most 226 high-level exact field operations, versus a dense elimination.  The route fits the cap but still demands many precise 31-bit modular products and inverses, which the oracle pool did not execute reliably even when the structural hint named the factorization.

## Worked demo

`make_instance(seed=0, n=2, group="toy")` renders in full as:

```text
Recover simultaneous discrete logarithms from exact collision identities.

All arithmetic in exponent equations is modulo the prime q=11.  Let G be the
subgroup of the nonzero residues modulo the prime p=23 generated by g=2; g has
order exactly q.  For unknown exponents x_1,...,x_2, each constrained by
0 <= x_j < q, the public keys are y_j = g^x_j mod p.

Public keys y_1,...,y_2, in that order:
  18 18

The paper's birthday construction turns equal group elements (collisions) into
linear equations in the unknown exponents.  Here n independent collision
equations have been composed into the displayed matrix equation

    A x = b (mod q).

Equivalently, row i certifies the exact group identity

    product over j=1,...,2 of y_j^(A[i,j]) = g^(b_i) (mod p).

The coefficient matrix A is nonsingular over GF(q).  Rows and columns below are
1-indexed; their order is significant, entries are ordinary decimal integers in
the inclusive range 0,...,q-1, and no exponent or answer entry may be omitted.

Matrix A (2 rows, 2 entries per row):
  1: 10 8
  2: 1 8

Right-hand side b_1,...,b_2:
  9 10

Find the unique vector [x_1,...,x_2].  Your answer must be a JSON list of
exactly 2 decimal integers, in public-key/column order, each in 0 <= x_j < q.

Give your final answer inside <answer></answer> tags, as the JSON list just specified.
Example: <answer>[0,1]</answer>
Output nothing else inside the tags.
```

The answer is `[6,6]`; subtracting the two equations modulo 11 quickly gives `x_1=6`, then `x_2=6`, so the demo is genuinely hand-solvable.

```python
>>> verify(inst, [6, 6])
(True, 'ok')
>>> verify(inst, [7, 6])
(False, 'public-key mismatch at coordinate 1')
```

## Caveats

- This is emphatically Track B.  Python, a CAS, or any modular linear-algebra package solves every instance almost instantly; the family is not evidence for discrete-log or cryptographic hardness.
- The public keys and group identities make verification faithful to the paper, but `A,b` already determine the answer.  The generator therefore measures the paper's post-collision linear-algebra step, not the cost of finding birthday collisions.
- G4 samples uniformly from the exact stated language `GF(q)^n`, so its exact density is `1/q^n`.  It says nothing about structured mathematical guesses; those are addressed only partially by G6 and the oracle runs.
- The hinted failures reveal an arithmetic burden as well as an invariant-recognition burden.  One hinted attempt was length-limited, so the 0/3 estimate is small and should not be overinterpreted.
- External CAS systems, optimized BLAS-style finite-field elimination, and symbolic rank-one detectors were not counted as failing attacks because they are expected to succeed; the exact Gaussian reference represents that tool-enabled regime.
- `canonical_key` is not a complete canonical labelling algorithm for arbitrary weighted bipartite matrices.  It hashes sorted row/column signatures and a determinant invariant, and is proven here only against equation reordering, variable/public-key reordering, group power automorphisms, and their compositions.
- The standard library is sufficient.  The two fixed subgroups are rechecked in `selftest` using deterministic 64-bit Miller–Rabin bases and exact order equations; no probabilistic primality claim is trusted at grading time.
