# Rejected: no certified hard distribution for approximate Gröbner bases

Paper: Gwen Spencer, [*Extended Hardness Results for Approximate Gröbner Basis
Computation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04472), arXiv:1605.04472 (2016).

## Decision

No generator module is shipped. The paper proves worst-case NP-hardness for two
fractional Gröbner-basis regimes, but it does not provide an answer-first sampler,
a theorem-backed construction with a Gröbner-basis certificate, or a distributional
hardness result. The proposed inverse family—start with a known ideal basis and add
redundant generators—can satisfy **G** and **V** only by moving to a special planted
distribution to which neither hardness theorem applies. It therefore fails **H** on
Track A. The paper also provides no solver-visible compact route that could support
an honest Track B family.

There is an additional verifier defect in the proposed triage: Gröbner conditions
plus reduction of the input generators by the candidate establish only one ideal
containment. Without explicit reverse-containment certificates, verification must
itself solve ideal membership, which is not the requested cheap witness check.

Per Step 0, I stopped before writing `gen_1605_04472.py`, a self-test report, a
README, or oracle transcripts. Those artifacts would imply that a generated
distribution had cleared gates that the paper does not support.

## The paper's exact search problem

Section 2 defines, for a polynomial set \(\mathcal F\) on variables \(X\),
\(\mathcal F_Y\) to be all input polynomials containing a variable from \(Y\).
The \(q\)-Fractional Gröbner Basis Problem asks for both:

1. a set \(X'\subseteq X\) with
   \(|\mathcal F_{X'}|\leq(1-q)|\mathcal F|\); and
2. a Gröbner basis, for a lexicographic order, of the ideal generated by
   \(\mathcal F\setminus\mathcal F_{X'}\).

Thus the witness is not merely a common zero, a satisfying Boolean assignment, or
a basis of some convenient subideal. It includes the allowed ignored-variable set
and a basis of exactly the remaining ideal.

Section 2.1's theorem “Extending Robust Hardness” (labelled `extfrachard` in
the source) proves that for every \(\epsilon>0\), the
\((7/10+\epsilon)\)-fractional problem is NP-hard for lexicographic orders even
when every input polynomial has degree at most 3 and involves at most 3 variables.
Section 2.2's theorem “Robust Hardness For Maximum Degree 2” (labelled
`extfracharddeg2`) proves the corresponding degree-2 result for
\(q=4/5+\epsilon\), again with at most 3 variables per polynomial. These are
worst-case reduction theorems, not claims about randomly planted inputs.

The first reduction starts from satisfiable Max Not-2. It adds Boolean polynomials
\(y_i(1-y_i)\) and degree-at-most-3 predicate polynomials whose zeros encode the
acceptable predicate totals. The second starts from satisfiable Max OXR and uses
Boolean polynomials plus degree-at-most-2 predicate polynomials. In both proofs a
hypothetical fractional Gröbner-basis algorithm is used to obtain a partial Boolean
assignment, which is extended by conditional expectation to contradict a
hardness-of-approximation theorem.

The direction matters: the proofs show **Gröbner basis \(\Rightarrow\) assignment**.
They do not show that knowing a satisfying assignment supplies the required
Gröbner basis or a certificate for either ideal containment.

## STEP 0: what produces the certificate?

For a general input, a Gröbner basis is produced by a Gröbner-basis algorithm such
as Buchberger's algorithm, which the introduction identifies as the standard
algorithm. That is the object whose robust worst-case hardness the paper studies;
running it inside `make_instance` would be solving the generated instance and would
violate G.

The proposed workaround samples a basis \(G\) first and manufactures \(F\) from it.
It has two forms, neither of which establishes the required hardness claim:

- If \(G\) remains among the emitted generators and the generator merely appends
  redundant multiples or combinations, the answer is exposed by the construction.
  Generator-aware selection, reduction, and pair tests are direct attacks.
- If \(G\) is hidden by a secret invertible combination or a sequence of ideal-
  preserving transformations, the generator knows a certificate, but the paper's
  NP-hardness theorems say nothing about this planted distribution. For linear
  combinations of a linear basis, Gaussian elimination recovers the ideal in
  polynomial time. For more elaborate transformations, recovering the hidden basis
  is a new average-case problem with no theorem or measured regime in the paper.

Starting instead with a planted satisfying Max-OXR or Max-Not-2 assignment does not
repair G for the native task: the assignment is a zero of the encoded system, not a
Gröbner basis of its ideal. Asking only for that zero would change the output problem
from fractional Gröbner-basis computation to planted CSP feasibility. Moreover,
Håstad's worst-case promise hardness used by the paper does not imply hardness for a
distribution obtained by sampling predicates conditional on a chosen assignment.

## Why the suggested verifier is incomplete

Suppose an answer contains an ignored set \(X'\), a lexicographic variable order,
and candidate polynomials \(G\). An exact checker can cheaply verify:

- the deletion budget and that the retained set is exactly
  \(F'=\mathcal F\setminus\mathcal F_{X'}\);
- Buchberger's criterion for \(G\), by reducing all S-polynomials by \(G\); and
- \(F'\subseteq\langle G\rangle\), by reducing every member of \(F'\) by \(G\).

Those checks do **not** establish
\(\langle G\rangle\subseteq\langle F'\rangle\). Reducing members of \(G\) by
the original, generally non-Gröbner set \(F'\) is not a valid ideal-membership test.
The answer would additionally need explicit identities

\[
g_j=\sum_i h_{ji}f_i\qquad\text{for every }g_j\in G,
\]

which the verifier could expand and compare exactly. General Gröbner-basis outputs
and these representation polynomials have no paper-provided bound compatible with
the 2,000-character/256-element answer cap. If the checker computes the missing
representations itself, the checker is invoking the same ideal machinery the solver
was asked to perform, so V is no longer cheap.

## Why Track B does not rescue this proposal

Track B requires an efficient reference algorithm, its complexity and measured
shipping cost, plus a solver-visible compact route requiring at most 300 exact
operations. General lexicographic Gröbner-basis computation is precisely the
non-polynomial-time task under study, so Buchberger's algorithm cannot honestly be
advertised as an efficient reference algorithm for the theorem regime.

Restricting the planter to linear ideals, univariate gcds, triangular systems, or an
explicit short transformation can make a polynomial-time reference algorithm
available, but that creates a new easy special family not analyzed by either main
theorem. If the short transformation is emitted, it exposes the answer; if it is
secret, it is the generator's private information rather than a compact route a
solver can infer from the instance. The paper contains no change-of-variables,
symmetry, or telescoping construction that bridges this gap.

## Easy regimes and scope boundaries found in the full text

- Section 2 states that once a lexicographic Gröbner basis of the finite Boolean
  variety is available, a point can be recovered efficiently by successive
  elimination, with only two values checked for each remaining variable. This makes
  a root a consequence of the certificate, not a way to construct the certificate.
- The conclusion says that the robust-hardness results are only for lexicographic
  orders. Analogues for other common term orders are open, and it notes that revlex
  and grevlex often behave much better empirically in Buchberger computation.
- The Strong \(c\)-partial results in Section 3 are conditional on unresolved
  coloring conjectures. They likewise do not provide a certified instance sampler,
  and using their explicit reduction without a source witness does not meet G.

## Gate outcome

| Requirement | Result | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| G — certificate known without solving | Possible only for a specially planted ideal; a planted CSP assignment alone is not the required basis |
| H, Track A — hard generated distribution | **Fail** | The two Section 2 hardness theorems are worst-case results and do not cover basis-first or planted-assignment distributions |
| H, Track B — costly efficient algorithm plus compact route | **Fail** | No efficient algorithm exists for the general theorem regime, and the paper supplies no solver-visible compression construction for an easy special regime |
| V — cheap exact witness verification | **Fail as proposed** | Gröbner checks and \(F'\subseteq\langle G\rangle\) omit reverse containment; explicit representation identities would be required |
| G4–G9 | Not run | Cardinality, attacks, and oracle failures cannot replace the missing distributional-hardness claim |

The paper supports a strong worst-case complexity statement. It does not support the
different claim required here: an unlimited answer-first distribution whose emitted
instances retain that hardness while carrying short, exactly checkable Gröbner-basis
certificates.
