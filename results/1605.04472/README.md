## Problem and trust model

The generator turns Gwen Spencer's [*Extended Hardness Results for Approximate Gröbner Basis Computation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04472) into an exact reduced-lexicographic-Gröbner-basis task over `Q`. The solver receives dense quadratic generators and returns the unique basis `x_j^2-a_j` as rational polynomial coefficient pairs. Verification uses exact rational reduction, the executable pairwise-coprime case of Buchberger's criterion, and exact matrix rank; it never reads `inst["answer"]`.

Generation is inverse. It samples the rational basis constants first, writes `b_j=x_j^2-a_j`, and emits shuffled generators `f_j=u_j sum_i(b_i)+d_j b_j`. Their coefficient matrix is `diag(d)+u*1^T`, with determinant `prod(d_j)*(1+sum_j u_j/d_j) != 0` because every `u_j,d_j` is positive. Hence the emitted generators and planted basis generate exactly the same ideal by construction.

## Why Track B, and what the paper says

Section 2.1, Definition 1 defines the `q`-Fractional Gröbner Basis Problem and explicitly says `q=1` is ordinary Gröbner-basis computation, which is the task used here. Section 2.1, Theorem 2 proves worst-case hardness for `q>7/10`, degree at most 3; Section 2.2, Theorem 4 proves it for `q>4/5`, degree at most 2. Both remain hard even with at most three variables per input polynomial. Those are worst-case theorems, not distributional guarantees, and this generator's dense polynomials have 32 variables, so this module does **not** claim Track A or the paper's strongest sparse-input restriction.

An efficient algorithm exists and is disclosed: treat each `x_j^2` as an unknown and use exact Gauss–Jordan elimination, `O(k^3)`. At the shipping preset it solved 8/8 instances in 8,752 mean counted exact operations (10,152 maximum), with mean wall time 0.0069 seconds in the final run (0.0033–0.0069 seconds across two runs). That is easy with software and out of reach by hand. The compact route finds the repeated background `u_j` and exceptional difference `d_j` in each shuffled row, normalizes to `a_j+(u_j/d_j)S=c_j/d_j`, sums all 32 equations to get `S=sum a_j`, and substitutes back. It takes exactly 224 counted operations, below the 300-operation cap, but still requires substantial exact rational arithmetic without tools. The paper's Introduction identifies Buchberger's algorithm as the general mechanical method; Sections 2.1–2.2 also note efficient successive elimination once a lex basis is available.

## Worked demo (`seed=0`)

This is the complete rendered demo instance:

```text
Compute the unique reduced lexicographic Groebner basis of an ideal over Q.

Definitions:
- Q is the field of rational numbers.
- Work in Q[x1,...,x3] with lexicographic order x1 > x2 > x3.
- A polynomial's leading monomial is its largest monomial in that order.
- A finite set G is a Groebner basis of I if G generates I and its leading
  monomials generate the leading-monomial ideal of I.
- G is reduced if every member is monic and no non-leading monomial in one
  member is divisible by another member's leading monomial.

Input encoding:
Each of the following 3 rows is [q1,...,q3] ; c and denotes
    q1*x1^2 + ... + q3*x3^2 - c.
All coefficients are exact. Fractions use num/den with positive denominator.
The input ideal I is generated by all rows; row order has no meaning.

1: [2,2,4] ; 27
2: [6,3,3] ; 87/2
3: [3,6,3] ; 46

It is guaranteed that the unique reduced basis is
    {x1^2-a_1, x2^2-a_2, ..., x3^2-a_3}.
Each a_j is a positive reduced fraction p/q with 8 <= p <= 15,
q in [2,3], and the a_j are pairwise distinct.

Output one JSON object with key "basis". Its j-th entry (1-indexed) must
be [[-p,q],[1,1]], the exact [constant, x_j^2] coefficient list for
x_j^2-p/q. Rational pairs must be in lowest terms with positive denominator.
The basis array must have exactly 3 entries; order matters and repeats are forbidden.

Give your final answer inside <answer></answer> tags as that JSON object.
Shape example for k=2 only: <answer>{"basis":[[[-8,3],[1,1]],[[-11,2],[1,1]]]}</answer>
Output nothing else inside the tags.
```

The answer is `{"basis":[[[-11,3],[1,1]],[[-9,2],[1,1]],[[-8,3],[1,1]]]}`. `verify` returns `(True, "ok")`; dropping its last polynomial returns `(False, "wrong basis length: expected 3 polynomials")`. A person can solve this demo on paper: the exceptional entry in each row identifies its variable, and summing the three normalized equations gives the shared total.

## Caveats

- This is a Track B no-tool benchmark, not evidence of average-case computational hardness. Exact elimination solves shipping instances in milliseconds.
- The paper's three-variable-per-polynomial hardness regime is not represented: expanding the invariant makes every emitted generator depend on all 32 variables.
- `0/200,000` is a structure-aware empirical rate under the declared prior (correct shape, reduced allowed fractions, distinct constants). It does not model algebraically informed guesses. The exact valid-answer count is one in the reported candidate space.
- The panel tests RHS rank, diagonal-only, zero-shared-total, prior-mean, and 256 random restarts. It does not benchmark an external CAS implementation of Buchberger, F4/F5, or every possible modular shortcut; exact Gaussian elimination is already the successful reference algorithm for this special coefficient-linear subclass.
- `canonical_key` deliberately solves the small exact linear system and sorts the unique basis constants. This is invariant under row ordering, row scaling, and variable relabelling, but it is a few-millisecond canonicalizer rather than a syntax-only hash.
- The identical 0/3 rates for hinted and placebo prompts are only a diagnostic from three models per arm. They may mean the arithmetic workload dominates, and should not be overread as proof that invariant recognition is irrelevant.
