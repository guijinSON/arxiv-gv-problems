# Rejection audit — arXiv:2112.02313

## Decision

The attempted bounded Kempe-reconfiguration family passes **G** and **V**, but it
does not pass **H/G9(c)** on either track.  The retained implementation is
`rejected_gen_2112_02313.py`; it is evidence for the decision, not a module to
ship.

## What the paper actually guarantees

Section 1 defines a Kempe change as swapping the two colors on one connected
bichromatic component.  Lemma 1.1 (Las Vergnas--Meyniel) proves that all
`k`-colorings of a `d`-degenerate graph are Kempe equivalent when `k >= d+1`,
but the paper explicitly says that the resulting sequence may be exponential
and that even a polynomial length bound in this general tight regime is open.

The paper's polynomial regimes do not cover the attempted shipping instances:

- Theorem 0.1 requires maximum degree `Delta <= k`; the 10 by 10 grid has
  `Delta=4` and uses `k=3`.
- Theorem 0.2 requires `mad(G) <= k-epsilon`; the grid itself has average degree
  `3.6`, so its maximum average degree is already greater than `3`.
- Theorem 0.3 requires `k >= tw(G)+1`; the 10 by 10 grid has treewidth `10`, not
  at most `2`.

Section 5 makes Theorem 0.3 constructive and gives an `O(tw*n^4)` algorithm when
a suitable chordal completion is supplied.  That algorithm is not applicable
to the attempted `k=3`, treewidth-10 distribution.

## G and V

Generation is valid composition of identities: the builder starts from a known
proper coloring, applies valid Kempe changes, and carries the resulting
16-change certificate through vertex and color relabelings.  It never solves
the endpoint instance.  Verification independently recomputes each exact
bichromatic component and compares the final coloring.  Fresh local tests gave
12/12 planted certificates, five distinct corruption rejections, and 0 valid
answers among 200,000 structure-aware random answer strings.

## Why Track A is not justified

Lemma 1.1 is a connectivity theorem, not a hardness theorem, and it says nothing
about the random-walk endpoint distribution generated by the module.  The best
evidence available is a node-limited search failure.  That is useful adversarial
evidence, but it does not establish that the generated distribution has no
efficient recovery method.  In particular, the planted route is random rather
than the consequence of an invariant or other compact structure a solver could
discover.

## Why Track B also fails

The proposed insight was to quotient the `2n` vertex/color descriptions by the
fact that all anchors in one bichromatic connected component describe the same
Kempe move.  The standard exact bidirectional BFS already uses exactly that
quotient.  At the 10 by 10 shipping preset, over eight seeds it generated
220,001 successors per instance (the explicit limit), expanded 4,215 states,
stored 92,389 states on average, took **3.027582 seconds per instance** in the
fresh audit, and solved 0/8.

Thus the **mechanical cost** is at least 220,001 component successors and the
**compact route length is not shorter**: the only proposed route is the same
component-quotient search.  The previously reported 225 operations counted
only the 16 component traversals and vertex recolorings needed to *verify an
already-known planted answer*.  It omitted the work of obtaining that answer,
which is the entire problem.  Once corrected, the intended-route count exceeds
the 300-operation G9(c) cap by more than two orders of magnitude.

No applicable efficient reference algorithm is known for these instances, so
declaring Track B would also be dishonest.  Moving to the paper's bounded-
treewidth or degree-bounded regimes would provide a polynomial algorithm, but
the paper supplies no separate short invariant-based route; for the natural
instances the certificate-producing algorithm and the solver's route are the same.

## External-loop note

This is not an oracle-based rejection.  A final bare `harden.py` run completed
two genuine attempts at the easy preset, both invalid, before the shared
OpenRouter key returned HTTP 403 on all redraws.  The harness therefore issued
no verdict.  The rejection above is the prior, deterministic H/G9 audit and
would remain unchanged if the missing oracle call completed.
