## The problem and why the certificate is trustworthy

The source is Swanson and Walker, [*Tensor-Multinomial Sums of Ideals:
Primary Decompositions and Persistence of Associated
Primes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.03545). An instance gives two dense monic
polynomials in disjoint variables, sets `I=(F(x))` and `J=(G(y))`, and asks for
one primary component of `(I+J)^N`. The answer is a normalized symbolic ideal
`((x-r),(y-s))^N`.

Generation expands two sampled sets of distinct integer roots; it never factors
the emitted polynomials. Definition 1.2 supplies the relevant filtration
condition. Lemma 2.5 gives the intersection identity, while Lemma 3.1 proves the
component primary because `(x-r,y-s)` is prime. Corollary 3.4 packages this
construction over algebraically closed fields; over `Q` the proof applies here
because every factor and every prime sum is already rational. The checker
substitutes `r,s` into `F,G`, checks nonzero derivatives (simple factors), and
checks the exact normalized sparse-polynomial schema. It accepts every valid
root pair, not only the planted pair.

## Why Track B

There is no honest Track-A claim. Corollary 3.4 constructs the decomposition
once filtered primary components are known, and the examples in Section 4 use
Macaulay2. More generally, exact polynomial factorization is algorithmic.

The measured reference algorithm uses an exact Sturm sequence to isolate one
integer root of each polynomial. With naive dense polynomial arithmetic its
cost is `O(n^3 + n^2 log B)` exact rational operations per polynomial. At the
designated shipping preset (`n=64`, `B=75000`) the final fixed G5 run used
**99,544 arithmetic operations and 1.45 s**; eight G6 runs averaged **118,312.75
operations and 1.68 s** under the same shared-host load. It succeeds 8/8, as a
Track-B reference should. The compact route
uses the top two Vieta/Newton
identities: an even arithmetic progression has a midpoint between its two
middle roots, and its variance determines the squared spacing. Recovering one
endpoint on each side and confirming both by exact Horner evaluation costs 296
arithmetic operations. Nothing in the bare statement announces that invariant.

Easy cases deliberately avoided are tiny root bounds, roots near zero, unit
spacing, and supplying the factor filtrations themselves. Any of those makes a
by-hand scan or direct use of the paper's formula immediate.

## Worked demo (`seed=0`)

This is the complete rendered demo; a person can solve it by factoring the two
quartics or by the Newton-moment shortcut.

```text
PRIMARY COMPONENT OF A TENSOR-BINOMIAL POWER

Work in the polynomial ring Q[x,y].  A polynomial term table below contains one line `k c` for the term c*x^k (or c*y^k); the lines are deliberately not sorted.  Missing terms are not allowed.  Thus
F(x)=sum_k f_k*x^k and G(y)=sum_k g_k*y^k.

Both polynomials are monic of even degree 4.  Each is guaranteed to have 4 distinct integer roots, all in the inclusive interval [-20,20].
Let I=(F(x)) and J=(G(y)).  We consider the ideal (I+J)^N in Q[x,y], where N=2.

For any integer roots r of F and s of G, the ideal Q=((x-r),(y-s))^N is a primary component of (I+J)^N.  Find any one such component.  The notation ((x-r),(y-s))^N means the Nth power of the ideal generated by x-r and y-s.

F TERM TABLE (exponent coefficient):
1 -3382
0 7920
4 1
2 539
3 -38

G TERM TABLE (exponent coefficient):
2 659
0 11880
3 42
1 4578
4 1

OUTPUT FORMAT
Return a JSON object with exactly the keys `generators` and `power`. The two generators must be in the fixed order x-r, then y-s.  Encode each sparse polynomial as a list of terms [[num,den],[e_x,e_y]], with the constant term first and the monic linear term second.  Denominators must be positive and reduced; here they are 1.  Set `power` to 2.  Order and normalization are part of the bounded certificate language; repeated generators are not allowed.
Give your final answer inside <answer></answer> tags, as exactly that JSON object.
Example: <answer>{"generators":[[[[-3,1],[0,0]],[[1,1],[1,0]]],[[[5,1],[0,0]],[[1,1],[0,1]]]],"power":2}</answer>
Output nothing else inside the tags.
```

One generated answer is:

```json
{"generators":[[[[-11,1],[0,0]],[[1,1],[1,0]]],[[[10,1],[0,0]],[[1,1],[0,1]]]],"power":2}
```

`verify(inst, answer)` returns `(True, "ok")`. Changing `x-11` to `x-12`
returns `(False, "x-r does not divide F exactly")`.

## Caveats

- The family promises split, distinct integer roots and samples a highly
  structured arithmetic-progression distribution. It is not evidence that
  generic primary decomposition is hard.
- The G4 prior is uniform over normalized bounded integer root pairs. It already
  enforces the output shape and power, but it does not model an algebra-aware
  factorization algorithm; that is why the successful reference algorithm is
  reported separately.
- The panel includes exact Sturm root isolation but did not implement modern
  modular/LLL polynomial factorization or call Macaulay2. Those methods may be
  faster in wall time; their existence reinforces the Track-B label, while the
  unmeasured constant-factor improvement is a limitation of the baseline data.
- Translation, reflection, term reordering, and swapping `x,y` were tested.
  `canonical_key` intentionally identifies instances with the same unordered
  pair of progression spacings under those symmetries.
- Most importantly, multi-vendor no-tool hardness and the three-arm hint effect
  remain unmeasured because the only available OpenRouter key is capped.
