# Rejected at Step 0: arXiv 2512.17821

Paper: Kyle Pratt, [*Cubes from products of terms in progression with one term
missing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17821) (2025).

## Decision

No self-contained family from this paper meets **G, H, and V** simultaneously.
The prior-triage proposal—choose progression parameters whose omitted product is a
cube—fails **G** as an unlimited native family and **H on both tracks**.  The
paper's main theorem is a complete classification: after the paper's required
normalization, there are only eight positive witnesses.  The remaining cases are
negative instances, but their completeness certificates are elliptic-curve rank,
Chabauty, and Mordell--Weil-sieve computations rather than bounded witnesses that
the requested checker can execute.  Positive witnesses themselves would pass V by
direct substitution.

This is a Step-0 rejection, so no generator was written and no oracle-loop or gate
files were fabricated.

## Exact native problem and classification

Theorem 1.1 asks for integers `n,d,y` satisfying

```text
product_{0 <= j < k, j != i} (n + j*d) = y^3,
5 <= k <= 11, 0 <= i < k, n*y != 0, d >= 1, gcd(n,d) = 1.
```

It proves that the complete list of `(k,i,n,d,y)` is

```text
(5,0,-14,5,6)   (5,0,-11,5,6)
(5,1, -8,3,4)   (5,3, -4,3,4)
(5,4, -9,5,6)   (5,4, -6,5,6)
(7,3,-10,7,120) (7,3,-32,7,120).
```

The `y` values above follow by exact substitution.  Thus only four `(k,i)` inputs
have any positive witness and each has at most two.  Reordering data, changing a
seed, or applying the reversal involution (3.2) does not create a new canonical
problem.  G8 would correctly collapse those variants, so they cannot supply an
unlimited sequence of distinct instances.

Corollary 1.2 is finite for the same reason.  Its one curve has six zero points and
only two nonzero rational points,
`(-17/7,120/49)` and `(-39/7,120/49)`.  Affine coordinate disguises are isomorphic
copies, not unrelated instances, and a correct canonical key must collapse them.

## The certificate-producing algorithm and its cost

For the only viable positive-witness proposal, the certificate-producing algorithm
is the classification table in Theorem 1.1:

1. compare `(k,i)` with the four populated keys;
2. return one of at most two stored triples `(n,d,y)`;
3. optionally verify it using one gcd and at most ten factor multiplications.

This is `O(1)` time and at most eight row comparisons plus twelve elementary exact
checks at the maximum native parameter `k=11`.  A local CPython timing of two
million dictionary lookups, including loop and indexing overhead, was 0.760 seconds,
or about **0.38 microseconds per lookup**.  There is no larger shipping size: the
theorem fixes `k <= 11` and the table is complete.

The compact route is the same table lookup: one keyed lookup (or the same at-most-
eight comparisons) and no mathematical derivation.  Consequently the mechanical
and compact costs are both constant and differ by fewer than roughly twenty simple
operations.  There is no Track B compression gap.  The answer prior is also tiny:
only eight witnesses exist globally and at most two for a supplied `(k,i)`, far from
the required unguessable answer space.

## Why neither track rescues the family

### Track A

Track A fails H.  Theorem 1.1 is a necessary-and-sufficient classification in the
entire parameter regime the paper treats.  A distribution generated by selecting a
listed solution is a lookup distribution, regardless of how hard the authors' proof
of completeness was.  Worst-case Diophantine difficulty outside `5 <= k <= 11`
does not establish hardness for this finite distribution.

### Track B

Track B also fails H.  For the generated distribution, the reference algorithm is
the constant-size lookup above, and the compact route is no shorter in any meaningful
sense.  Scaling a listed solution by a cube gives infinitely many equations, but it
changes `gcd(n,d)=1` to a nontrivial common divisor and therefore leaves the paper's
exact native problem.  Even if that normalization were relaxed, Euclid's algorithm,
division by the gcd, and the same eight-row lookup recover the witness in
`O(log H)` bit operations; the compact route performs exactly those operations.

Affine disguises of Corollary 1.2 likewise do not help.  In factored form, six root
differences reveal the translation and scale with a sort and a handful of gcd or
rational operations, followed by the same two-point lookup.  In expanded form they
remain coordinate changes of one fixed curve and fail canonical diversity.  A costly
general Chabauty computation would not be an honest reference cost for this generated
distribution when direct normalization is available.

## Why the negative and coefficient-vector routes fail V

Most `(k,i)` pairs are negative cases.  “No solution” is not itself an executable
witness.  Sections 2--4 establish it using rank-zero elliptic curves, a finite
coefficient-vector search, and, for `k=7,i=3`, elliptic-curve Chabauty over
`Q(cuberoot(5))` plus a Mordell--Weil sieve (Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.1).  The
paper does not provide a bounded Nullstellensatz-style refutation or another compact
certificate whose validity can be checked by substitution and exact arithmetic.

Section 2 reports nearly fourteen million incomplete coefficient vectors for
`k=11,i=4`, which initially looks promising for Track B.  It does not yield an
admissible witness family.  A triple of indices whose associated ternary cubic has
cube-free part in the displayed set `L` certifies impossibility only because Lemma
2.3 imports precomputed elliptic-curve rank-zero facts.  A checker that merely tests
membership in `L` appeals to the theorem instead of checking the claimed
nonexistence.  Requiring the rank/Chabauty evidence makes verification depend on the
same research-level machinery and fails V's cheap executable-witness requirement.

## Gate summary

| requirement | outcome |
|---|---|
| G — unlimited native generation | **Fail:** eight normalized positive witnesses; coordinate/reversal variants are canonical duplicates. |
| H — Track A | **Fail:** Theorem 1.1 reduces the generated distribution to constant-size lookup. |
| H — Track B | **Fail:** mechanical lookup and compact lookup have comparable constant cost; no compression gap. |
| V — positive witness | Pass by gcd and exact product/cube comparison. |
| V — negative witness | **Fail:** completeness relies on rank, Chabauty, and sieve computations not carried by an executable bounded certificate. |
| Overall | **Rejected at Step 0.** |

The relevant sources are Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 for the finite
classification; Lemma 2.1 and Definition 2.2 for coefficient vectors; Lemma 2.3 for
the rank-zero obstruction; Section 2's final two paragraphs for the roughly
14-million-vector computation; and Proposition 4.1/Lemma 4.2 for the Chabauty case.
